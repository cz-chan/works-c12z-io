Spotlife was an early-stage social network built around discovering, sharing and saving places through user-generated content and recommendations. I joined as a Growth Product Manager, but being part of a small team meant that the role quickly expanded beyond growth. I ended up working across product, analytics, research and strategy, treating the product as a connected system rather than a set of isolated problems.

One of the first things I worked on was building the product analytics infrastructure from scratch with Amplitude. I defined the event taxonomy, user properties and tracking strategy, coordinated the implementation with engineering and created dashboards so the team could understand what was happening inside the product. This also meant documenting the framework behind the analytics, so it could continue to be useful as the team grew.

A large part of the work was understanding why people behaved the way they did. I conducted user interviews and qualitative research to map how people discovered, saved and shared places, both inside and outside Spotlife, as well as how they traveled and created content. Those conversations helped uncover behavioral patterns that influenced decisions around acquisition, activation and retention, rather than forcing users into predefined flows.

I also worked closely with Data Science to turn AI image capabilities into actual product features, defining data requirements and user flows around image recognition, better spot discoverability and automated field completion.

Alongside this, I developed the broader growth strategy, connecting acquisition, activation and retention loops with UGC and recommendations, and tying those loops to the product's B2B monetization model. On the acquisition side, the social content strategy I set up brought in 12% more traffic.

Where tooling was the bottleneck, I built it myself. I wrote internal tools in Python and JavaScript to make uploading and retrieving content easier for the marketing and product teams, and for the creators themselves, cutting out steps that were slowing everyone down.

Onboarding was the other big piece. I redesigned the experience from its information architecture through to the number and purpose of each screen, looking for ways to reduce unnecessary friction and improve activation. The resulting changes contributed to a 17% improvement in early retention.

What made Spotlife particularly interesting was that growth wasn't really a separate function from product. Understanding how people behaved became the starting point for deciding what to build, how to position it and how to create the loops that could make the product grow.
