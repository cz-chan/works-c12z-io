I joined Spotlife, a social network for discovering, sharing, and saving places with user-generated content, as a Growth Product Manager. My first goal was to design from scratch how the platform would grow: acquisition, activation, and retention loops for users and businesses, with the latter serving as the B2B monetization layer. This laid the foundation for knowing where to go and how to get there.

The next logical step was to find a way to track user behavior within the product, so I built the product analytics infrastructure from scratch using Amplitude and created the corresponding documentation so that the entire team could understand it:

- I defined the event taxonomy, user properties, and tracking strategy,
- I coordinated the implementation with the engineering team, and
- I created dashboards to understand what was happening within the product.

With Amplitude, I knew **what** users were doing, but to understand **why**, I needed qualitative data. So I had to roll up my sleeves and—just like at every company I’ve worked at—get out there and talk to them. I conducted interviews about their social media habits and travel behavior, and held user testing sessions where I recorded how they used the actual product. That revealed weaknesses that Amplitude didn’t pick up on and allowed us to adapt the product to how they behaved—rather than the other way around.

Some of those weaknesses weren't in the product itself but in the workflows around it, so where tooling was the bottleneck, I built it myself: internal tools in Python and JavaScript that made uploading and retrieving content easier for creators and for the marketing and product teams, improving their experience and saving hours of manual work.

With all this knowledge acquired and organized, I was able to work closely with the data science team to use this data to our advantage and feed our AI models so they could understand users’ different contexts and improve their interpretation of various images, thereby enhancing the algorithms for content recommendations and spot discovery.

All of the above served as the foundation for improving onboarding and, as a result, increasing activation and, consequently, retention by ~17%.
